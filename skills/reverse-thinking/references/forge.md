# Forge the endgame (Working Backwards)

Write the announcement of the finished thing before anything is built, then let the hardest
customer and builder questions test it. A press release nobody would care about means the concept
is not ready, and learning that now is a win.

Draft every section yourself from evidence instead of interviewing the user: cycle draft →
challenge it out loud → revise. Collect the questions only the user can answer and bring them all
at once at the end.

**Concept type** sets the language. A product has customers, cost, and competitors. An internal
tool, civic or public-sector service, or open-source project has users or residents, effort to
adopt, the current workflow it replaces, and sustainability. Pick one before drafting.

## 1. Pain first

Capture four things, each with its source or labelled unknown:

- **Who:** a specific person or role, not everyone.
- **Problem:** concrete and felt, in their words where a source records them.
- **Stakes:** what happens to them if nothing changes.
- **Today:** how they cope now and what they have already tried.

Start from recorded pain: the project's problem statement, user or citizen voices, support
records, data. When the context opens with a solution or a technology, trace it back to whose pain
it serves; a technology is a how. Done when all four are filled.

## 2. The press release

Date it at launch and write it for the person in step 1.

| Section | What it forces |
| --- | --- |
| Headline | What this is, in one sentence that person would understand |
| Subheadline | Who benefits and what changes for them |
| Opening | What is announced, for whom, and why they should care |
| Problem | Their pain, felt without mentioning the solution |
| Solution | What changes for them, not what was built |
| Leader quote | The vision beyond the feature list |
| How it works | The experience from their side |
| User quote | Something a real person would say |
| Getting started | A concrete path to first value |

Hold every sentence to these bars: the words the user would use; a specific fact in place of each
superlative; survives "so what?"; honest, because the FAQs will expose overselling. The headline
and subheadline become the endgame sentence.

## 3. Customer FAQ

Become the busy, skeptical user who has been burned before. Write six to ten questions covering how
this differs from what they do today, trust (their data, continuity, who is behind it), effort to
adopt, edge cases from real use, and the one question the team hopes nobody asks. Each question is
an objection standing between interest and adoption.

Answer each honestly and specifically; mark each answer evidenced, inferred, or unknown. When an
answer exposes a gap in the concept, classify it: blocker, follow-up, or accepted trade-off.

## 4. Internal FAQ

Become the builders' panel: engineering, operations, the owner, whoever funds or approves it. Write
six to ten questions covering feasibility (the hardest unsolved part, dependencies), viability or
sustainability, resources and a realistic timeline, what kills it, why us and why now, what we say
no to, and the question the builder avoids. Scale them to the actual team: a solo hackathon entry
and an enterprise programme face different questions.

An honest unknown is a valid answer when it carries what it would take to find out and when the
answer is needed. Push hardest on resources and timeline, the answers most often optimistic.

## 5. Verdict

Sort what survived into three tiers:

- **Holds:** clear, specific, and defended by evidence.
- **Needs work:** right direction, not yet deep enough.
- **Cracks:** risks, contradictions, or gaps that could sink the concept, each with what it would
  take to address it.

Then write the endgame record. When the concept collapses, say so plainly and name what to forge
next instead.

## Reply shape

Target and branch line → pain record → endgame record → verdict tiers → the user-only questions →
two or three directions, one of which is usually to plan toward this endgame and then audit that
plan. The full press release and both FAQs go to the scratchpad file.
